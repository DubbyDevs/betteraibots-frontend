How to Build a Content Creation Service Using Google's Free Pomelli Tool

While the AI world obsesses over ChatGPT and Claude, Google quietly released a tool that's enabling a new type of content service. Pomelli—a free, experimental tool from Google Labs—scans any business website and generates a complete brand DNA, then produces on-brand marketing content in seconds. Here's how entrepreneurs are using it to build content creation services and the complete technical playbook for implementation.

Understanding the Market Problem Pomelli Addresses

Small businesses face a persistent challenge: they know social media presence matters for credibility and customer acquisition, but creating consistent, on-brand content requires skills and time most don't have.

The traditional approach to maintaining an active social presence requires multiple skill sets: brand strategy, graphic design, copywriting, and social media management. A business owner trying to do this themselves faces a weekly gauntlet: photographing products or services, editing images to match brand aesthetics, writing captions that reflect their brand voice, designing posts that work across multiple platforms, and scheduling everything consistently. The alternative—hiring professionals—typically requires significant monthly investment.

Most small businesses choose a third option: they do nothing. Their social accounts sit dormant for months, which creates a credibility problem. When potential customers research a business and see the last Instagram post was from six months ago, they may question whether the business is active or established. This drives customers to competitors who appear more current, even if the actual quality of service is identical.

This is where the opportunity emerges. Small business owners often know they should be leveraging AI tools, but they don't know how to translate AI capabilities into actual business applications. They've used AI for personal tasks (recipes, travel planning, email drafts) but can't bridge the gap to using it systematically for content creation and marketing. This knowledge gap creates space for service providers who understand the tools.

The Pomelli System: Technical Overview

Pomelli operates on a straightforward premise: if a business has a website, that website contains everything needed to understand and replicate their brand identity. The tool uses AI to analyze the visual design, copy, imagery, and positioning of a website, then extracts what it calls "Business DNA"—essentially a comprehensive brand book generated in under two minutes.

Access Pomelli at labs.google.com/pomelli. It's completely free and doesn't require any technical setup beyond a Google account. The interface guides you through three core functions: Generate Business DNA, Get Campaign Ideas, and Generate Creatives.

Phase 1: Generating Business DNA

The Business DNA feature is the foundation of the entire workflow. Input any business website URL, and Pomelli will analyze and extract:

  • Visual Identity: Logo files, primary and secondary brand colors (with exact hex codes), typography choices, and the overall design aesthetic
  • Imagery Library: Curated photos pulled from the website that represent the business's visual style
  • Brand Voice: Tone characteristics (professional, casual, luxurious, approachable, etc.) derived from website copy
  • Messaging Framework: Core taglines, value propositions, and positioning statements
  • Business Overview: A synthesized description of what the company does, who they serve, and how they differentiate

This output is genuinely comprehensive. Professional brand strategists typically spend considerable time creating brand books of this caliber. The fact that Pomelli generates this in minutes demonstrates the power of the underlying AI analysis.

The strategic use of Business DNA is as a demonstration tool. You don't need permission to run a public website through Pomelli—it's analyzing publicly available information. This means you can proactively create brand DNA documents for prospective clients before ever speaking with them. Take a screenshot of the generated brand DNA (showing their colors, fonts, tone analysis, and value propositions) and use it in your initial outreach.

This approach works because you're leading with value rather than a sales pitch. You've already done work that demonstrates both your capabilities and the tangible output of working with you. The business owner sees their brand professionally documented in a way they may not have seen before.

Phase 2: Campaign Ideation

Once Pomelli has generated the Business DNA, it moves into campaign ideation mode. Based on the business's industry, positioning, and brand characteristics, Pomelli suggests campaign concepts that align with their marketing context.

For example, if you analyze a local fitness studio, Pomelli might suggest campaigns like:

  • "Your Strongest Year Yet" (New Year wellness campaign)
  • "Find Your Workout Community" (emphasizing the social aspect of group fitness)
  • "Transform Your Lunch Break" (targeting busy professionals with midday class options)

Each campaign comes with strategic framing: the core message, target audience considerations, and key selling points to emphasize. This isn't randomly generated content—it's strategically aligned with the business's actual positioning and market.

The value here is positioning yourself as providing strategic thinking, not just content creation. When you present campaign ideas to a prospect, you're demonstrating understanding of their business context and marketing opportunities.

Phase 3: Creative Asset Generation

This is where Pomelli becomes a production tool. For each campaign concept, it generates a complete set of marketing assets:

  • Social Media Posts: Image-based posts formatted for Instagram, Facebook, and LinkedIn with on-brand design
  • Carousel Posts: Multi-slide posts that tell a story or showcase multiple offerings
  • Ad Creative: Promotional graphics designed for paid social advertising
  • Copy Elements: Headlines, captions, calls-to-action, and social proof statements

Every asset is rendered in the business's actual brand colors, uses their logo and imagery style, and reflects their tone of voice. The consistency is what creates professional output—it looks cohesive rather than generic.

The assets are editable and downloadable. You can adjust copy, swap images, or modify layouts before delivering to clients. This is important because while Pomelli's output is strong, you'll occasionally want to customize elements to better fit a specific promotional need or correct a minor misalignment with the brand.

Building Your Service Offering: The Content Pack Model

The most scalable approach is offering structured "content packs"—a defined number of posts delivered on a recurring schedule. Here's how successful operators are structuring their offerings:

Starter Package:

  • 20 branded social media posts per month (5 per week)
  • Delivered in weekly batches
  • Posts formatted for Instagram and Facebook
  • Basic captions included

Professional Package:

  • 40 posts per month (roughly 2 per day)
  • Multi-platform formatting (Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, Google Business)
  • Strategic campaign theming (monthly promotional focus)
  • Custom caption writing based on promotions or seasonal events

Premium Package:

  • Daily posting (60+ posts per month)
  • Multiple posts per day for high-traffic accounts
  • Includes scheduling and posting service (client never touches it)
  • Monthly strategy calls to align content with business goals
  • Ad creative for paid campaigns included

The structure allows clients to choose based on their needs and posting frequency preferences. The work doesn't scale linearly—creating 20 posts versus 40 posts takes roughly the same amount of time once you've generated the Business DNA and campaign concepts.

The Client Acquisition Strategy: Value-First Outreach

Cold outreach typically fails because you're asking for something (a meeting, their time, their attention) before providing any value. The Pomelli strategy inverts this dynamic—you provide value first, which naturally leads to conversations about working together.

Here's the proven outreach sequence:

Step 1: Generate Their Brand DNA

Before contacting a prospect, run their website through Pomelli. Screenshot the generated Business DNA—particularly the sections showing their brand colors with hex codes, tone analysis, and value propositions. This takes two minutes.

Step 2: Create 2-3 Sample Posts

Generate a campaign concept relevant to their business, then create 2-3 actual social media posts they could use immediately. Download these as image files. This demonstrates the end product they'd receive.

Step 3: Craft Your Outreach Email

The email should feel personal and helpful, not salesy:

Subject: Quick brand analysis for [Business Name]

Hi [First Name],

I was researching [industry] businesses in [city] and came across [Business Name]. I ran your website through my brand analysis tool and thought you might find this interesting—it instantly pulled your brand colors, tone, and positioning into a structured overview.

I also created a couple of sample social posts using your actual branding (attached). These are ready to post if you want to use them.

If you're interested, I can send over the full brand DNA document I generated. I work with [industry] businesses to create consistent, on-brand content like this on a monthly basis.

Let me know if you'd like to see more.

[Your Name]

The psychological power of this approach is that you've already done the work. You're not asking for anything—you're offering something they can use immediately. This typically increases response rates compared to generic "I can help your business with social media" outreach.

Step 4: The Follow-Up Sequence

If they don't respond within 3-4 days, send a brief follow-up:

Hi [First Name], wanted to make sure you saw the sample posts I created for [Business Name]. No worries if you're not interested—just wanted to follow up in case it got buried in your inbox.

If they respond positively, send the full brand DNA document and propose a quick 15-minute call to discuss a content package. On the call, your goal is to understand their current social media situation (lack of time, inconsistent posting, difficulty creating professional content) and position your service as a potential solution.

Scaling Through Lead Generation: Using Gemini for Prospecting

Finding qualified leads consistently is often the constraint for service businesses. Here's where Google's Gemini becomes a prospecting tool. Because Gemini is deeply integrated with Google Search and Maps data, it can generate business lists based on location and industry.

The Gemini Prospecting Prompt:

"I'm running a digital marketing agency specializing in content creation for local businesses. Build me a prospecting list of 20 real, active businesses in [specific industry] in [city/region]. For each business provide: business name, website URL, industry category, and decision maker name if available. Focus on businesses that would benefit from regular social media content—things like local services, restaurants, fitness studios, tour operators, retail stores, professional services."

Gemini will generate a list with real businesses and their details. Verify the information by quickly checking the websites, then add these to your outreach spreadsheet. The advantage over manual research is speed—you can generate a list of 20 qualified prospects in 5 minutes versus spending an hour manually searching and compiling.

Advanced Prospecting Prompts:

Once you've exhausted obvious local businesses, get creative with Gemini queries:

  • "Find businesses in [city] that have Instagram accounts with fewer than 1,000 followers but appear to have quality products/services. These are likely businesses that want social presence but aren't consistently posting."
  • "Identify businesses in [industry] that were recently featured in local news or won awards. These businesses are growing and may need better marketing support."
  • "Find [industry] businesses in [city] whose websites show they've been recently redesigned. Website redesigns often indicate investment in marketing and potential receptiveness to new services."

The key is building a systematic prospecting workflow: generate 20-30 leads weekly, run the top 10 through Pomelli to create sample assets, send personalized outreach to prospects. Maintaining consistent prospecting cadence is essential for building a pipeline.

Fulfillment and Automation: Using GoHighLevel

Landing clients is one part—you also need to deliver the service efficiently. This is where GoHighLevel becomes valuable. It's an all-in-one client management and social media scheduling platform that allows you to manage multiple clients from a single dashboard.

Core GoHighLevel Features for This Service:

1. Multi-Platform Social Scheduling

Connect each client's social accounts (Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, Google Business, TikTok) to GoHighLevel. You can schedule posts across all platforms simultaneously, weeks or months in advance. This is more efficient than logging into each platform separately.

2. Client Account Connection via SMS

Instead of asking clients for usernames and passwords (which creates security concerns and friction), GoHighLevel lets you send a connection link via text message. The client clicks it, authorizes their social accounts, and you're immediately able to schedule content. This reduces onboarding friction considerably.

3. Content Calendar View

See all scheduled posts for all clients in a calendar view. This makes it easy to ensure consistent posting schedules and avoid gaps in content delivery.

4. Built-in AI Content Generation

If you need to supplement Pomelli's content or customize captions, GoHighLevel has integrated AI for generating social copy and even creating images. This gives you a backup content source without leaving your workflow platform.

5. Client Portals and Reporting

Create branded client portals where businesses can log in to see their content calendar, approve posts before they go live, and view performance metrics. This transparency builds trust and reduces support requests.

The Monthly Fulfillment Workflow:

Once you've landed a client, here's the systematic process for delivering their monthly content:

Week 1 (Monthly Planning):

  • Review the client's upcoming promotions, events, or seasonal focus
  • Generate 2-3 campaign concepts in Pomelli that align with their monthly priorities
  • Create the full set of posts for the month (20-40 depending on package)
  • Download all assets and organize in a folder labeled with client name and month

Week 1-2 (Scheduling):

  • Upload all posts to GoHighLevel
  • Schedule them across the month according to optimal posting times (typically 9-11am and 5-7pm for engagement)
  • Write or customize captions for each post, including relevant hashtags and calls-to-action
  • Set everything to auto-post so the content goes live without your intervention

Ongoing (Monitoring):

  • Check notifications for comments or messages on scheduled posts
  • If client has approval rights, send weekly previews of upcoming posts
  • Track basic engagement metrics to report monthly performance

With this system, the delivery becomes systematized. Most of the work is in the initial setup and monthly planning—the day-to-day fulfillment is automated.

For business owners, this time has real value beyond just the hourly rate—it's time they can spend on serving customers, developing products, or growing the business.

Common Implementation Pitfalls

Pitfall #1: Over-Promising Results

Don't promise that your posts will "get them more customers" or specific engagement metrics. Social media results depend on many factors outside your control (product quality, pricing, competition, audience size). Instead, promise consistent, professional brand presence and time savings. Improved results may come, but make them a bonus rather than the primary value proposition.

Pitfall #2: Underpricing to Win Clients

It's tempting to offer very low pricing to make the decision easy. However, low pricing often attracts price-sensitive clients who may demand the most work and have the highest churn rates. Position yourself as providing professional service at appropriate rates. You may land fewer clients initially, but they'll typically be better clients who value your work.

Pitfall #3: Not Setting Boundaries

Clearly define what's included in each package and what costs extra. Unlimited revisions, custom video editing, responding to customer comments 24/7—these scope creep items can destroy your efficiency. Put clear boundaries in your service agreement and maintain them.

Pitfall #4: Manual Posting

Never agree to manually post content by logging into client accounts yourself. It doesn't scale, and it creates liability if something goes wrong with their account. Always use GoHighLevel's scheduling or have clients connect their accounts through official integrations. This keeps everything professional and scalable.

Building Systems for Growth

Once you've proven the model with your first several clients, you can scale beyond your personal capacity by systematizing and potentially hiring:

  • Virtual Assistant: Handles outreach, lead research using your Gemini prompts, and initial prospect qualification
  • Content Coordinator: Runs websites through Pomelli, downloads and organizes assets, and loads content into GoHighLevel
  • Account Manager: Handles client communication, monthly planning calls, and ensures content aligns with business goals

You remain in the strategic role—making final campaign decisions, closing sales, and overseeing quality—while a team handles execution. This allows you to serve more clients while maintaining quality.
