What is World: The Vision Behind the Technology

World (formerly known as Worldcoin until its October 2024 rebrand) is a revolutionary biometric identity verification platform that uses iris-scanning technology to create a global network of verified humans. Developed by San Francisco and Munich-based Tools for Humanity, World's mission extends far beyond simple identity verification—it aims to build "the world's largest identity and financial network as a public utility, giving ownership to everyone."

Core Components of the World Ecosystem

World ID: The cornerstone of the platform, World ID is a privacy-preserving global identity network that enables users to verify their "humanness" online while maintaining privacy through zero-knowledge proofs. Think of it as a digital passport that proves you're a real person without revealing personal information.

The Orb: World's flagship hardware device is a distinctive spherical biometric scanner that captures iris images to create unique digital identifiers. Designed by Thomas Meyerhoffer, the Orb symbolizes the company's vision of global unity.

World App: The first wallet application supporting World ID creation, allowing users to securely store their digital identity, manage digital assets, and access various integrated applications.

WLD Token: A utility token with governance properties that empowers users by giving them a say in the protocol's future. Orb-verified World ID holders are entitled to claim recurring grants of free WLD tokens.

World Chain: The platform's blockchain infrastructure, launched in 2024, designed to support the growing ecosystem of verified humans and applications.

The Genesis of an Idea

The concept for World emerged from a prescient understanding that AI would soon make it impossible to distinguish human-created content from machine-generated material. The solution required something uniquely human, universally accessible, and cryptographically secure—iris scanning.

How World Works: The Technology Explained

The Orb: Engineering Marvel Meets Functional Design

Latest-generation Orbs, powered by Nvidia Jetson processors, are five times more powerful than their predecessors while being smaller and more efficient. Verification typically completes in ~30–45 seconds with encrypted transmission and local deletion.

Privacy-First Architecture

  • Zero-knowledge proofs
  • Local processing and deletion
  • Decentralized verification
  • End-to-end encryption
  • Voluntary participation

Revolutionary Face Auth Technology

Face Auth introduces one-to-billion comparisons for higher-assurance authentication, capable of distinguishing even identical twins.

The Universal Basic Income Vision

Privacy Concerns and Regulatory Responses

Privacy paradox: biometric sensitivity vs. privacy-preserving implementation. Measures include ZK proofs, local processing, decentralized verification, open-source components, and user control.
